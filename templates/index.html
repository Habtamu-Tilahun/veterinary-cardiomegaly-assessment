AI-assisted Assessment of Cardiomegaly in Dogs and Cats

Heart disease is a leading cause of mortality in aging dogs and cats, with cardiomegaly being one of the most common radiographic findings. Cardiomegaly can be assessed from thoracic radiographs by evaluating clinically established cardiac biomarkers, including Vertebral Heart Size (VHS) and the Cardiothoracic Ratio (CTR). However, manual assessment of these biomarkers is time-consuming, labor-intensive, and subject to measurement errors and interobserver variability, particularly in veterinary settings with limited access to specialized radiology expertise. This AI-powered veterinary cardiomegaly assessment system automatically segments cardiac and thoracic anatomical structures, performs quantitative analysis, and estimates VHS and CTR based on the extracted anatomical measurements. By providing accurate, objective, and clinically interpretable results that closely align with expert radiologist assessments, the system assists veterinarians in the early detection and monitoring of heart disease while improving efficiency, consistency, and confidence in clinical decision-making.

Vertebral Heart Size (VHS)

VHS estimates heart size by measuring the cardiac long and short axes on a lateral radiograph and normalizing their sum against the length of the fourth thoracic vertebra (T4).

Cardiothoracic Ratio (CTR)

CTR measures the ratio between the maximum horizontal width of the heart and the maximum horizontal width of the thoracic cavity on a ventrodorsal radiograph, providing another important indicator of cardiomegaly.
